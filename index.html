The Role of Artificial Intelligence in Biodiversity Conservation and Climate Change Mitigation

By Raheela Ramzan · BS Artificial Intelligence and Climate Change

Abstract

Biodiversity loss and climate change are interconnected environmental challenges. Artificial intelligence (AI) can help researchers analyze large datasets, monitor ecosystems and identify patterns that support environmental decision-making. This article reviews selected applications of AI in wildlife monitoring, habitat assessment, climate analysis and conservation planning, while recognizing limitations such as data quality, cost, energy use and the need for expert validation.

1. Introduction

Biodiversity—the variety of life at genetic, species and ecosystem levels—supports ecosystem functions and human well-being. Climate change, land-use change, pollution and overexploitation place increasing pressure on natural systems. AI methods can assist environmental researchers by processing satellite imagery, camera-trap photographs, acoustic recordings and climate datasets.

2. How AI supports biodiversity conservation

  • Wildlife monitoring: Computer vision can help classify animals in camera-trap images.
  • Habitat assessment: Machine-learning methods can analyze satellite imagery to identify land-cover changes.
  • Species identification: Image and sound recognition can assist species surveys.
  • Conservation planning: Data-driven models can help compare possible conservation priorities.

3. AI and climate change mitigation

  • Climate analysis: AI can help analyze complex weather and climate datasets.
  • Renewable energy: Forecasting models can support solar and wind energy planning.
  • Emissions monitoring: Data analysis can help identify and estimate patterns in greenhouse-gas emissions.
  • Ecosystem restoration: Environmental data can help assess locations for restoration projects.

5. Benefits and limitations

AI can make some monitoring tasks faster, support analysis across large areas and help researchers detect patterns. However, results depend on representative, accurate data. Systems may be costly, require technical expertise and consume energy. Predictions should be checked against field observations and expert knowledge, and conservation decisions should include local communities.

6. Future directions

Future work can combine remote sensing, ecological field studies and transparent AI methods. Research priorities include improving data quality, evaluating model performance across regions and ensuring that technology serves conservation goals.

7. Conclusion

Artificial intelligence is a promising support tool for biodiversity research and climate action, not a replacement for ecological expertise or effective environmental policy. Responsible use, reliable data and collaboration between scientists, communities and decision-makers are essential to achieving meaningful outcomes.
